Your Android. Your Games. Your Console.

Orbit is a free, controller-first gaming frontend for Android, designed to bring your games together in one place and make the experience feel like a dedicated gaming console.

The idea is simple. Connect your Android device to a television, grab a controller, and enjoy your games through one clean interface.

Orbit is built around the idea that your gaming experience should be at the center of everything.

No advertisements. No subscriptions. No paid features.

Just your games.

Why I'm Building Orbit

I've been a gamer since I was a kid.

Over the years, I've owned or played just about every gaming systems you can imagine having in the US. I've also built up a pretty extensive collection of games, including backups I've been keeping for nearly two decades.

These days, I'm a middle-aged industrial worker with a wife, a family, a full-time job, and probably more projects than I have time for.

But I still love gaming.

I've always wanted a way to bring my collection together into one place. Something I could keep connected to my television, pick up a controller, and just play without constantly switching between launchers, emulators, and applications.

There are some great gaming frontends out there, but none were quite what I had in mind.

So I figured, why not build my own?

I'm a mechanically inclined guy who enjoys building things and figuring out how they work. I'm learning the software side as I go, with some help along the way when I run into things I don't know.

I consider it a team effort, and I'm enjoying the process of learning something new.

What started as something I wanted for my own gaming setup has grown into a project I think other people might enjoy too.

So I'm making Orbit available to everyone, hoping other gamers can get some use out of it.

And if nothing else, I'm having a pretty good time building it.

What Is Orbit?

Orbit is an Android gaming frontend designed primarily for televisions and game controllers.

The goal is to bring Android games, supported PC games, and eventually older console games together under one interface.

I'm putting a lot of effort into making Orbit look and feel like a dedicated gaming system rather than a traditional Android launcher.

Development is ongoing. Some features are already working, while others are still being built or tested.

I'd rather take my time getting things right than rush out something that only works half the time.

---

Key Features

Orbit is being built around a few simple ideas:

- Controller-first navigation designed for television gaming.
- A clean Home screen with recently played games.
- Organized game libraries and Favorites.
- Game artwork and details.
- Support for discovering and launching games from compatible sources.

Some integrations are still in development, and supported functionality will continue to expand.

---

System Architecture

Orbit is currently built as a single Android application with two main components.

Orbit Home

The frontend responsible for everything you see and interact with, including the Home screen, game libraries, artwork, navigation, and settings.

Orbit Core

The underlying systems responsible for game discovery, library management, storage integration, and supported game launching.

Both components are being developed together, with an emphasis on reliability and keeping the interface separate from the underlying game-management logic.

Orbit uses native Android functionality where possible and is being designed to work with compatible third-party applications rather than replace them.

What's Planned?

I've got plenty of ideas for Orbit, but the main priorities are improving the interface, expanding game compatibility, and making the entire experience usable with a controller.

Future plans include better emulation integration, controller-based file management, and custom animations that give Orbit its own personality.

The Hardware Project

Alongside Orbit, I'm also working toward building my own dedicated Android gaming console.

The current hardware is a stripped-down OnePlus 13 with 16GB RAM and 512GB storage, housed in a compact enclosure with active cooling, external NVMe storage, and television connectivity.

The hardware is currently housed in a 3D-printed, Raspberry Pi-sized case inspired by the Steam Machine.

I've already experimented with repurposing smartphone hardware, so I know the basic idea can work.

While Orbit is currently being developed to run on standard Android installations, the long-term goal is to build it around a custom Android operating system specifically tailored to the hardware project.

The plan is to develop a custom AOSP-based GSI that makes better use of the hardware and provides a more integrated console experience. Eventually, I'd like Orbit to feel less like an application running on a phone and more like the operating interface of a dedicated gaming system.

That doesn't mean abandoning standard Android support. I still want people to be able to install and enjoy Orbit on their own compatible devices. The custom OS would simply be the version built specifically for my hardware.

There's still a lot to figure out, but that's part of the fun.

For now, my priority is getting Orbit's software right.

Keep an eye out for the hardware project. I'll share more as it develops.

Project Status

Orbit is currently in active development.

The interface is designed primarily for 1920×1080 television displays, and I'm continuing to test functionality and compatibility on Android hardware.

Some features are unfinished, and there will undoubtedly be bugs along the way.

Check the Releases section for available builds, supported functionality, and known limitations.

Supporting Orbit

I intend to keep Orbit free.

This started as a personal project, and I don't want people to have to pay to enjoy it.

Development does cost money, though, especially when hardware and testing are involved.

Eventually, I'd like to offer an optional way to support the project through GitHub Sponsors or Ko-fi.

There won't be advertisements, subscriptions, or features locked behind donations.

If you don't want to contribute financially, that's perfectly fine. Using Orbit, reporting bugs, and sharing feedback helps too.

Feedback & Community

I'm building Orbit around what I've always wanted in a gaming frontend, but I'd love to hear what other people think.

Bug reports, compatibility feedback, and constructive suggestions are welcome through GitHub Issues.

Just remember that I'm one guy working on this in my spare time.

I've got a full-time job, a family, and responsibilities outside this project, so development will move forward as time allows.
